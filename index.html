The mission

A different way to look at NFTs.

Physical antiques help us understand earlier generations. Digital artifacts may one day help future collectors understand the culture, creativity, and technology of our own era. My goal is to research that possibility carefully—not to label every old token a treasure.

The founding idea
“What if we stopped treating NFTs as disposable digital images and started preserving them as artifacts of our digital civilization?”

Digital Antiquities is an independent collecting and research concept focused on discovery, documentation, and long-term preservation. It begins with curiosity, evidence, and respect for digital history.

Research approach

What makes an NFT worth investigating?

I’m interested in overlooked digital collectibles that may have meaningful origins. No single factor proves value; the strongest candidates are those whose history and characteristics can be checked.

01

Early provenance

Investigate verifiable creation or mint history and how close an item is to the early use of its contract or collection.

02

Genuine scarcity

Examine token supply and available evidence. A small supply can be relevant, but scarcity alone does not create demand.

03

Authenticity

Look for a traceable contract, token ID, collection origin, creator information, and supporting records.

04

Historical context

Ask whether an item represents a meaningful moment, style, community, or phase of digital culture.

05

Preservation

Check whether the media and metadata remain accessible and whether their storage has a credible long-term plan.

06

Real market evidence

Separate genuine collector activity and completed sales from asking prices, speculation, and promotional claims.

Principles I stand for

Research honestly. Collect thoughtfully.

Trust matters more than making an item sound rare. I want the story behind a collectible to be understandable, inspectable, and clearly separated from speculation.

What I aim to document

  • Contract address, network, and token ID
  • Creation or mint date, when verifiable
  • Supply and provenance evidence
  • Collection context and available metadata
  • Known uncertainties and missing information

What I will not promise

  • Guaranteed appreciation or future resale
  • That every old or rare NFT is historically important
  • That a listing price proves market value
  • Historical claims that the evidence cannot support
  • That research removes the risks of NFT ownership

Important: Digital Antiquities is a research and collecting philosophy, not a guarantee of investment performance. NFTs can be illiquid, volatile, difficult to resell, or lose value entirely. Always verify an item's records and assess your own risk before buying.

The long-term vision

From discovery to a digital archive.

The project can grow step by step—starting with research into existing NFTs, then building documented case studies, a transparent classification approach, and eventually a community that values digital preservation.

Phase 01

Discover

Search for overlooked candidates and record why each one merits a closer look.

Phase 02

Verify

Check the available dates, supply, creator context, and transaction evidence.

Phase 03

Archive

Publish clear notes that distinguish verified facts from interpretation.

Phase 04

Preserve

Build a community around digital history, responsible collecting, and lasting documentation.
